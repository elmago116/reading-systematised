ABSTRACT 
Governments are more and more adopting Citizens’ participation 
processes since they may help better understand needs  and better 
reach communities’ goals. With the emergence of ICTs, cit izens’ 
participation processes took new forms such as social media, 
blogs and participative platforms. Nevertheless, although citizens’ 
participation outstands a great value for governments, its 
implementation raises several issues. Based on a review of th e 
literature, this paper identifies a typology of issues regarding the 
implementation of CP processes. This paper is an attempt to open 
new research aven ues on citizens’ participation through the 
multidisciplinary typology of issues it proposes. As well, drawing 
on two categories  of issues (citizens’ and technology issues), a 
context-based citizen participation approach is proposed that is 
based on three main concepts: context -based reasoning, spatio -
temporal representation, and visual analytics. Further em pirical 
studies need to be established to assert the relevance of the 
proposed approach.

1. INTRODUCTION 
The objective of citizen participation (CP) is to strengthen the 
involvement of citizen s in decision -making processes about 
meaningful choices that will affect their community [2]. However, 
and despite its potential to enhance participatory democracy, CP 
may not be representative of all citizens and consequently reaches 
opposite objectives t o what it was intended for  [51]. With the 
emergence of information and communications technologies 
(ICT), CP was transformed to become electronic participation (e -
participation). The use of ICT is intended to facilitate the 
participation process, make it m ore inclusive and ensure the 
engagement and involvement of stakeholders in a productive and 
constructive way [52, 9].  With e -participation, the CP process 
took new electronic forms such as social media, blogs, and 
forums. In addition, different public pa rticipation platforms have 
been impl emented around the world such as  “Votepour.ca” in 
Canada or “Présence citoyenne” in France.  
As stated above, although public participation outstands a great 
value for cities and communities, its implementation raises several 
challenges and issues [2, 9]. In  fact, the intertwining and 
interpenetration among technological and social processes [12] 
within organizations and  society, has led many researchers and 
practitioners to question the relevance of the process while 
considering the technological context in which the actors evolve. 
Therefore, various models and approaches have been proposed in 
the literature [32, 35] in order to increase the social value of 
participatory processes and ensure the ir progression in a 
transparent and a legitimate way. Transparency means that 
whether the provided information is accurate, accessible, 
understandable, complete, timely and free  or low -cost [43]. 
Legitimacy refers to whether if the process  is approved by a 
majority of adult population that is representative of the 
community [35]. In addition, many studies have denoted the 
multidisciplinary nature [9, 20, 46] of the concept and, therefore, 
suggested that "interdisciplinary" [45] or "transdisciplinary" [20] 
studies are required to enhance participatory processes in practice. 
[53] noted that the disciplines that influence the most the concept 
of CP are "political philosphy" and "political science" . More 
precisely, several theories , that could explain aspects or 
phenomena related to e-Participation, were identified [53] such as 
Foucault's theory of discourse analysis (1989)  [18], Ha bermas's 
critical theory (1994)  [23] and Actor Network Theory ANT of 
Latour (2005)  [29]. This paper identifies various issues and 
challenges regarding the implementa tion of citizens’ participation 
(PP) processes. To this end, a literature review has been 
conducted to identify these issues and challenges. A typology of 
public participation issues has emerged, underlying six major 
categories of CP issues and that  are: ethical issues, efficiency and 
cost-effectiveness issues, political issues, quality issues, citizen’s 
issues, and technology issues . Drawing on the citizens’ issues 
category, a new participatory approach is proposed combining 
three main concepts that are: " context-based reasoning", " spatio-
temporal representation" and "visual analytics".

2. Citizen participation and electronic 
participation 
According to [2], citizens’ participation has emerged since the 
years 1950 -1960 in the United States as part of Urban 
Development and Poverty Reduction Programs. The main goal of 
this concept i s to increase the level of citizens’ engagement in 
decision-making process, in order to enable them with a 
significant influence on decisions concerning their cities and 
communities. [16] defines citizen participation as "a process in 
which the ordinary pe ople of a community exercise power over 
decisions relating to general affairs of their community." 
Since one of the main principles of CP is to strengthen the 
engagement of actors whose power sources are limited, various 
typologies based on the level of in volvement have been proposed 
in the literature. [5] denotes that a low rate of citizen engagement 
in a participatory process leads to “ manipulation”. In this context, 
several studies depict ed low rates of citizens’ involvement [35, 
46, 19]. Hence, most of the proposed approaches aim to enhance 
these rates  in an attempt to reach the highest level which is 
defined by [5] as the level of “citizen power ”. [28] note that, 
under favorable conditions, citizens could participate more 
effectively and significantly i nfluence the decision -making 
process. One of these approaches is the adoption of ICT in order 
to enhance CP, which leads to the concept of e -participation. [32] 
define e -Participation as “ the use of information and 
communication technologies to broaden and  deepen political 
participation by enabling citizens to connect with one another 
and with their elected representatives ” (p.85 ). Actually, the 
integration of ICT in participatory processes is not an end to itself, 
but just a mean to achieve traditional goa ls of CP. Therefore, e-
participation maintain s the same goals than traditional CP while 
bringing new forms of communication, with the aim to increase 
the involvement of citizens and helping them better achieve their 
communities’ objectives. Indeed, one of the major challenges of 
e-Participation is to ensure that the true interests are expressed 
through the participatory process, including those of actors who 
are excluded from decision -making because of institutional 
inequalities or other forms of power rela tionships in society ([11] 
p.29).  
The use of ICT, and the different communication channels such as 
dedicated platforms, blogs, or social media platforms , brings new 
challenges for data collection and data analysis  [53]. Hence, 
several technologies may be required, as stated in [11], that could 
have particular relevance to participatory processes such as “ real-
time feedback ”, “ visualization” and other “ collaborative 
technologies”. These technologies could help build common 
understanding of context and probl ematics and facilitate 
interaction among citizens and other stakeholders [11, 7].

3.1 Methodology 
This literature review has been conducted mainly in the following 
databases: Science direct, ACM  Digital Lib rary, Springer Link, 
IEEE Xplore  and Web of Science . Key words used are e -
Participation, citizen participation, online engagement, public 
engagement, public participation, electronic participation and 
issue, barrier, challenge. The relevant papers to this literature 
review are those that depict, highlight and/or discuss challenges, 
issues or barriers to the implementation of CP processes. This 
research was done specially on journal and conference papers on 
the period of 2000 -2016. A first pass on paper titl es, then a 
second pass on papers abstracts, and finally a third pass through 
papers contents in order to select only relevant papers for this 
literature review.  Since the literature review established in this 
paper is not a systematic one, only 57 articles  was retained due to 
their relevance to our research objective and to the saturation of 
issues identification. A meticulous analysis of these papers was 
established in order to extract major issues and challenges for the 
implementation of participatory pro cesses. First, issues were 
identified per paper, resulting on a table that contains references

drawn regrouping issues, references and a column for the sentence 
indicating the issue in each paper. 17 issues have been depicted

convergence to a same category. For example, issues related to 
citizens such as involvement, influence and living context 
converged to the category “citizens’ iss ues”. Likewise, issues 
related to technology converged to the category “technology

3.2 Citizen participation categories of issues 
As stated in Section 2, e-CP is defined as: the use of information 
and communication technologies to broaden and deepen pol itical 
participation by enabling citizens to connect with one another 
and with their elected representatives ” [32] p.85. From this 
definition, there are mainly two t actors who are citizens and 

organizations. Each of these two actors may face different 
challenges and issues as they are taking part to the  participative 
processes.  
For organizations, CP introduces a new governance model in 
which citizens are part of the decision -making process. However, 
this new governance model may lead to different politica l issues 
as stated in  [52]. Furthermore, organizations need to prepare and 
to manage a CP process. To this end, organizations need to have 
the required budgets for the implementation of the process. 
Consequently, they face budget challenges with regards to  the 
reduction of costs that several governments around the World are 
putting in place [49, 35]. Moreover, CP brings transparency 
challenges since citizens will talk about public issues for which 
governments should release data in order to enlighten c itizens in 
their participation [42] . Finally, the success of a CP process 
depends on how the process itself is managed. To this end, 
organizations have to ensure that the citizens have the right 
information in order to adequately contribute to the process, and 
have also to ensure that the voice of the citizens is well 
understood by decision-makers. Hence, quality challenges have to 
be considered in the implementation of a CP process [11]. 
For citizens, the success of the CP process requires a high level of 
involvement from citizens; the process should be representative of 
all citizen profiles as possible so that to reflect a real image of 
citizens’ concerns. These challenges, related to citizens, nee d to 
be managed in the process [15, 60]. Finally, with the emerg ence 
of the e -participation process by using ICTs, several challenges 
may raise such as the digital divide where a portion of the 
population is represented in the process, and where focus is more 
put on technology than on people. These challenges need also  to 
be addressed [26, 39].  
We recognize that other issues from both sides may exist but these 
ones emerge from a first overview of the literature. Figure 1 
depicts these issues. The papers that were used in this literature 
are summarized in Table 1. The p apers are grouped with regard to 
their methodology: literature review, quantitative analysis, and 
qualitative analysis. Papers are published between 2000 and 2016. 
The different identified issues are discussed in what follows. 
3.2.1 Ethical issues 
The issues of trust, transparency and ope nness are frequently and 
extensively discussed in the literature  [40]. Indeed, these issues 
are interrelated in the sense that one could influence the other  
[19]. In the context of a CP process, citizens who do not trust 
their go vernment would actively resist the state, and therefore 
“are less likely to participate across all means ” ([22] p.335). 
Consequently, government agencies have to ensure that the 
process is transparent. To this end, agencies can ensure the 
transparency of t he process by at least consideri ng the following 
eight elements : " notice and agenda; minutes; closed meeting 
sessions; how public; video and audio recordings; electronic 
meetings; violations, penalties, fines, and attorneys' fees; and 
physical space "([42] p. 138). However, transparency is not only 
related to the process but it is also related to the data released by 
government agencies to help citizens having the right information 
for a good participation. This information has to be relevant in 
terms of us ability, accuracy, accessibility, completeness and 
understandability, and that it should be provided timely and free 
or at low cost [43]. 
3.2.2 Efficiency and cost-effectiveness issues 
In the current context of global economic crisis, performativity is 
becoming increasingly important in development agendas [31]. In 
this context, efficiency and cost -effectiveness goals take a 
primordial importance among states and governments priorities. 
In public participation field, criterion of cost -effectiveness has 
been defin ed in the evaluation framework proposed by [49] to 
ensure if the participatory process is cost -effective in some way. 
Similarly, [52] have specified the character istic "Low-benefit 
high-cost " of some participatory processes, through a review of 
the of leg islative and policy initiatives that took place in the last 
decade rand that are r elevant to public participation in European 
policy-making. As an example, the authors mention ed the case 
where participation were perceived as “ unnecessary by the 
population concerned for a relatively simple process” ( p.6). 
However, for more complex issues, increased participation is 
required to help states better respond to people and communities’ 
needs. Studying sustainability in public participation, [35] 
considers that a s ustainable participatory process is the process 
that is  able to reduce operational costs or at least to keep them 
invariant with respect to the previous (non participatory) process  
it replaces . Finally, one of the challenges that need to be 
considered in e-participation process is the access to technological 
infrastructure that remains relatively expensive [39].  
3.2.3 Political issues 
One of the main issues in CP is: "who participates - as well as 
who is excluded and who exclude them? "( [15], p. 275). This 
question is important to answer in order to give credibility to the 
process. In fact, the elite of the society can alter  the process  by 
dictating and orient ing the process since it can always influence 
decisions about the communities [26]. This elite can also s ee a 
decrease in the power that it has on the society where some 
powerful stakeholders “might become more wary of the process if 
they feel that their power is being diminished ” ( [11] p.30) and 
would seek to subvert deliberative efforts . Hence, the CP proce ss 
should not marginalize people and should ensure the involvement 
of poor and marginalized people in decisions that affect their 
communities [15]. Consequently, under favorable conditions, 
citizens could participate effectively and could significantly 
influence decisions concerning their communities [28]. 
3.2.4 Quality issues 
Misunderstanding of real problematics 
Problem identification is a crucial step in a participatory process. 
It requires a sound understanding of citizens’ living context  and 
requirement in order to attract their interest to the process. In fact, 
one of the barriers to citizen participation is “ the inadequate 
NGO’s understanding of local socioeconomic context ” ( [14] p. 
432). Consequently, if the  identified problems are relevant and 
representative of citizens’ reality, they would engage in 
participatory processes. Problems identification is one of the 
perquisite to the success of the process as stated in [49]: A good 
"Task definition" may ensure a good understanding of the scope 
and nature of th e participation task. This could reduce sources of  
conflict and confusion that may influence the effectiveness and 
credibility of the procedures set up in a participatory process. 
 
 
The need for in depth quality analysis and coherent evaluation 
frameworks 

Each CP process needs to be evaluated in a rigorous way as stated 
in [4, 1, 11, 33, 49]. This evaluation should include the impacts of 
the process on the decisions taken by officials.  This evaluation 
method can be based on a set of criteria to determine ho w 
organizations implement participatory programs and to measure 
the impact of these metrics on challenged policies and decisions  
[11]. The evaluation can allow depicting good practices and errors 
from the current participatory experi ences and that can be a  way 
to improve future processes [33]. 
3.2.5 Citizen issues 
Involvement 
Citizens’ involvement in participatory processes is a key element 
for the success of a CP initiative. Through the involvement and 
empowerment of citizens, civic participation opens up avenue s of 
co-production, which would increase the potential of participatory 
approaches to improve citizens’ quality of life of and better 
achieve communities’ objectives  [19]. Citizens’ involvement 
should be considered as early as possible in participation process.  
The "criterion of early involvement" is proposed by [49] to ensure 
citizens’ involvement from the first steps of a CP process.  
Although one of the goals of public participation is to increase the 
number of engaged citizens, their representativeness  regarding 
their communit ies represent a critical issue [35] . Citizens’ 
heterogeneity in terms of intentions, goals, knowledge, and social 
and socio -economic levels  represent a crucial point on which 
researchers and practitioners should focus to make CP a success. 
Acknowledging that measuring the heterogeneity of citizens is not 
a trivial task, research in this context should be conducted in order 
to develop new metrics. 
Influence 
A c itizen’s involvement in a participatory process does not 
necessarily mean that his voice will be heard  [15]. Hence, one of 
the main challenges is to measure the degree of influence that 
citizens have had on a decision -making process. To this end, [49] 
identified “the criterion of influence” to evaluate if the output of 
the process has a real impact on the decision-makers through the 
participatory process. 
Nevertheless, even if citizens are willing to be involved in CP 
processes, they may lack of time [1]. In fact, time can also be 
considered as a major constraint since for exampl e d ue to time 
constraints, few citizens are able to give their opinion in 
municipal assembly [19]. But, technology may play a role in order 
to overcome the time constraint by offering various new 
alternatives such as forums or social networks where people could 
express their opinions at any time. 
To this end, [15, 19 ] consider that it would be relevant for 
researchers to focus on questions, "(1) Who Participates? (2) How 
do they communicate and make decisions? (3  What influence do 
they have over the resulti ng public decisions and actions ? "([19] 
p. 514) in future studies. 
Citizen’s living context 
Citizens’ participation is generally intended to involve citizens in 
decision-making processes affecting their cities and communities. 
As discussed above, citizens’ involvement represents often amajor 
challenge for governments, given the very low rate of involvement 
noted by researchers and practitioners in practice.  Among 
challenges regarding the success of participatory processes 
implemented in Europe, authors in [13] denoted the importance of 
considering communities’ priorities while implementing 
participatory processes: "  the topics discussed were sometimes 
distant from people’s daily problems and priorities, so that 
content contributions by non experts was inhibi ted” (p.2). 
Therefore, we believe that the context analysis is a crucial step in 
the implementation of a citizen parti cipation process. In this way, 
[11] suggest that: “ effective public participation processes are 
grounded in analyzing the context closely.” [11] p. 1-2).

3.2.6 Technology issues 
The focus on technological aspects  
In the era of ICT, several scholars highlighted that  new e -
Participation approaches are more focusing on technology rather 
than on  social, human and socioecono mic aspects [53, 22]. 
However, it remains that the citizen is at the core of an e -
participation process since he is the entity that will feed the 
process with its proper input. Consequently, e -participation 
approaches should not only focus on technology bu t also on non 
technological aspects [17, 4, 39, 36] among others). By focusing 
on both technological and non-technological aspects, e -
participation will enhance the creation of "social value" [36] 
through the implementation of technologies dedicated to cit izens. 
Hence, the use of new technologies in a participatory process is 
not an end in itself, but a mean to facilitate citizens’ involvement 
and to help them make their voices heard . In this context , [57] 
rose the question related to the risk of “social un acceptability” of 
technologies and acknowledged that this risk may negatively 
impact public participation. 
The digital Divide 
As stated, e -participation refers to the use of ICT as a mean that 
citizens can use in order to express their opinions about subje cts 
related to their life. However, the use of ICT is not always 
accessible to all citizens; part of the citizens do esn’t have or 
doesn’t know how to use ICT  [59]. In this context, we are talking 
about the digital divide that is the disparity to use ICT in  different 
spheres of life between citizens: those who can and those who 
cannot [22]. Hence, digital divide can be considered as a barrier 
to electronic participation: not representative of all citizens.  
 
The lack of exclusive e-Participation technologies 
One of the questions that may raise for e -participation is: is there 
a specific technology dedicated to e -participation? [53, 8] note 
that there is no specific technology to public participation. In fact, 
nowadays adopted solutions in e -participation are based on a  
unidirectional communication between citizens and public sector 
organizations in the sense that citizens put their comments 
without having a visibility on how their comments are impacting 
decisions. Hence, it becomes imperative according to [8] to 
develop exclusive participatory technologies that address various 
issues and challenges related to e -participation and that were

4. A conceptual model for a context-based 
eParticipation approach  
In section 3, a multidisciplinary typology of CP issues has been 
proposed and argued, presenting six major categories which are 
ethical issues, efficiency and cost -effectiveness issues, political 
issues, quality issues, citizens’ issues and technology issues. 
Actually, this is not surprising s ince several scholars denoted the 
multidisciplinary nature of the CP field [9, 20, 46] and suggested 
that “interdisciplinary” [20] studies could enhance participatory 
processes in practice.  
We estimate that the proposed typology presents two potential 
contributions. First, it confirms and argues the multidisciplinary  
nature of the field (through the different categories of issues it 
depicts; political, technological, ethical etc.). Second, it opens up 
new research avenues since it could be  taken as a start  point to 
study the issues as well as the links that could exist between them. 
Moreover, it could be used by scholars from several disciplines to 
reinforce the understanding of issues and to propose 
interdisciplinary approaches that would enhance CP.  
Responding to the calls of [9, 20,46] , the conceptual model 
proposed in this part of the paper tries to combine concepts from 
two disciplines; social and technological, which are represented in 
our typology through “citizens’ issues” and “technology issues”  
categories. The first category of issues is composed of three main 
issues which are; “involvement”, “influence” and “citizen’s living 
context” (see section 3. 2.5). The second one is also composed of 
three main issues  that are ; “ the focus on technological asp ects”, 
“the digital divide” and “the lack of exclusive e -Participation 
technologies” (see section 3.2.6) . Hence, d rawing on these two 
categories of issues (“citizens’ issues” and “technology issues”), a 
conceptual model for a  context-based citizen particip ation 
approach is proposed that is based on three main concepts: 
“context-based reasoning ”, “spatio-temporal representation ”, and 
“visual analytics”. The proposed model is innovative since, and to 
the best of our knowledge, it is the first model that encom passes 
these three components in a single e -participation model. Hence, 
it can be seen as a  dedicated solution to e -participation. In 
addition, by centring the model on both citizens and technology, 
the proposed research model will overcome some of the iss ues 
that was mentioned in the literature, as depicted in Section 2, and 
that state that e -participation models are only focusing on 
technology (see section 3.2.6). 
As [53] argue: « The focal point of e -Participation is the citizen  » 
(p. 408), our model is citizen-centric. Thus, the starting point for 
this approach is the issue of “ citizen’s living context” (see section 
3.2.5.). Many scholars highlighted this issue as a crucial challenge 
on which researchers and practitioners should focus, in order to 
ensure that participation processes fit the context in which they 
are taking place. In this context, [13] argue that one of the reasons 
citizens do not participate is the fact that “ topics are distant from 
their daily problems and priorities ” (p.5). Authors in [ 11] 
distinguish between “ involvement” and “ influence” in a CP 
process and consider that “being involved  in a process  is not 
equivalent to having a voice” (p.278).  

As well, the model we propose in this paper  aims to increase the 
ability of citizens to take  part in participatory processes 
(«involvement") and to effectively influence decisions that affect 
their communities and cities (« influence”) [15].  
The proposed approach distinguishes three major actors involved 
in the CP process; “ citizens”, “ local aut horities” and “ other 
stakeholders: Non Governmental Organisations (NGO)” (see 
figure 2). A CP process that is based on the conceptual model 
presented in this paper woul d have two different inputs: fro m 
citizens and from local authorities. Citizens’ input consists on 
opinions of citizens expressed and analyzed through the platform. 
Local authorities’ input consists on relevant information provided 
to citizens regarding the CP process, its progress and the resulting 
projects and decisions (budgets, resources,  feedbacks etc.). 
Regarding other stakeholders such as NGOs, their role is a crucial 
one, since it would enable to  control processes and to increase 
their legitimacy and their integrity as recommended by [15]. 
Finally, as CP aims to help decisions makers t o make effective 
decisions regarding citizens and their concerns, the output of the 
CP process are decisions (see figure 2). 
Hereafter the relevance of each concept introduced in the 
conceptual model is argued. 
4.1 The context-based reasoning concept 
In this p aper, we used the following definition for the context: 
“Contextualization: implies the analysis of human agents and 
(non-human) while involving  their interests, their challenges and 
their degree of convergence ". [57] 
As stated in section 3.1.5, the citiz ens’ living context is 
recognized as an important issue that should be considered in the 
implementation of participation processes. For instance, [8] 
considers that participatory technologies offered to citizens should 
be aligned with heir goals, their soc ial context, their intentions 
and their capabilities of learning and adopting. Similarly, [11] 
argue that participation processes are grounded in analyzing the 
context closely.  
For that, our approach is based on “context -based reasoning” in 
an attempt to respond to scholars’ calls regarding the considering 
of citizen’s con text in participatory processes. Moreover, the 
introducing of this concept aims to help analyzing the context 
closely in order to enable effective participation processes  [11]. 
[15] distinguish between two important measures or variables that 
could attest of the effectiveness of CP which are “ involvement” 
and “ influence” of citizens on decision making in CP processes . 
Thus, we can say that analyzing the living context closely of 
citizens c ould enhance CP processes in an attempt to increase 
their involvement and their influence on these processes.  These 
two measures represent two major issues regarding the citizen 
participation in CP processes (see section 3.2.5).  Actually, this 
causality relationship (between the considering of context and the 
increasing of involvement and influence)  needs to be further 
analyzed through qualitative and quantitative studies to test its 
relevance. By making CP processes grounded on real citizens’ 
living contex t, our approach suggest that this would probably 
enhance citizens’ involvement and could reinforce their influence 
on decision making. For instance, [13] argue that one of the 
reasons citizens do not participate is the fact that “ topics are 
distant from pe ople’s daily problems and priorities ” ([13] p.3). 
Thus, daily problems and priorities could be better understood 
through analyzing the citizens’ living context closely.  
In our approach, the contextualisation would aim to reflect, as 
much as possible, the context of life of citizens .  Through the 
introducing of context -based reasoning in CP processes, this 
would probably enhance both the understandings of governments 
and citizens about real problems and priorities of communities.  
[10] considers that in a c ollaborative work, it is supposed that 
each participant understands how others make decisions and 
follows the steps of their reasoning to reach decisions. In fact, in 
face-to-face collaborations, participants use different contextual 
information to argue t heir ideas. In an electronic collaboration, 
these contextual data need to be made explicit, in order to enhance 
both citizens and governments understanding of real problems and 
priorities. Thus, the introduction of context -based reasoning in 
participative processes would help city planners to elaborate 
topics that are closer to people’s daily problems and priorities 
[13]. 
4.2 The spatio-temporal representation 
concept 
Actually, what we would add to contextualization is the fact that 
an opinion refers also to a location and a time. When a citizen 
expresses an opinion, although it is somehow related to his living 
context, it is mandatory to specify any space and at any time this 
opinion concerns. Otherwise, it would not be relevant to speak 
about opinions in absolute terms. 
In other words, an opinion could be considered as a variable that 
evolves as well as locations and time evolve. For time, it is 
obvious to argue its evolution. For locations, its evolution could 
be argued by the fact that context and its charact eristics could 
change over time and thus opinion about the context would 
change too. 
 Specifically, in CP, the involvement of citizens in decision 
making aims to better reach citizens goals which are both 
reflected and affected by their context. Thus, a pa rticipative 
process intends an evolution of the citizens’ location to happen 
somewhere (for example improving infrastructure or building 
entertainment). Through citizen participation, governments aim to 
improve citizens’ quality of life and better achieve communities’ 
objectives [19]. Thus, the second concept included is our context -
based model for CP is spatio -temporal representation. In fact, 
contextualization of spatial data is integrated in the proposed 
approach to enhance the characterization of citize ns’ life context 
through the using of “the spatio -temporal representation” concept. 
This concept is supported by the Geographic Information Systems 
(GIS) technology. Actually, several authors argue the relevance of 
GIS as an ICT area that play an important  role in e -Participation 
tools and initiatives [53, 34, 17, 45]. To this end, authors in [34, 
17, 45] investigated the potential of digital mapping to make 
smarter and more effective participatory initiatives. [34] indicate 
that the appropriation of geogra phical information technology by 
the general public refers to the emergence of a knowledge 
production model which is based on the contribution. Indeed, 
accessibility and ease of use of spatial and geolocation 
applications use increases the tendency of user s to access and 
produce relevant spatial knowledge, allowing them to better 
interact and understand the space they share [6]. 
In our approach, the contextualizing of a district would assign him 
a characterization which gives a clearer visibility to citizen s and 
policy makers on the living situation of the citizens of this district, 
all based on the data provided by the citizens in this location. The 
features could be "parking problems, unemployment rate, crime 

rate, socio-cultural category, drug rates, etc.". The characterization 
may be made by street, by district, by sector etc. Hence, the 
context-based reasoning is introduced to enable the definition of 
contextual attributes that would reflect, as well as possible, the 
context of living in a location. Furt hermore, it would enable a 
better understanding and a better expression of citizens' needs, 
real problems and priorities. The reference point that would 
enable this contextualization is then the location (spatial data).  
The question to ask here is; since our approach is citizen -centric 
and aims to help both citizens and organisations to better 
understand real priorities, how could these two actors contribute, 
be involved and have a greater visibility of processes and projects 
progress to asses if they are really fitting to their real context or 
not? This questions leads as to the third concept used in the 
proposed approach that is “spatio-temporal visualisation” which is 
argued hereafter. 
4.3 Visual analytics (spatio-temporal 
visualization)  
An answer to the qu estion asked above proposed in this paper 
could be the integration of the visual analytics concept in the 
proposed context -based CP approach in order to enable spatio -
temporal visualization and real -time tracking of opinions in their 
context. First, as vis ual analytics are offered through Geographic 
Information Systems technologies, the technical integration of this 
concept in the approach would be simple and achievable. Second, 
potential of visualization has been argued by many authors in the 
literature. F or instance, [17] note that visualization plays a 
relevant role in e -participation as “argument tools”. Thus, this 
concept is integrated in an attempt to provide greater visibility to 
citizens about the progress of processes and projects in their 
locations. In this sense, the visual monitoring of projects starts 
from the collection of public inputs until completion of the 
project, and this by means of spatio-temporal visualization.  
Citizens could then view and track project status at any time, from 
the lau nch of the participatory process until completion of the 
project. This feature aims to enhance the sustainability and 
legitimacy of the participatory process over time, through 
notification, project monitoring, historical, cartographic 
visualization of cities before, during and after the implementation 
of projects etc. as recommended by [35, 36]. In addition, 
visualization would track with transparency the state of cities 
before, during and after the decision making process [50]. In fact, 
according to [35, 36], citizens need to be aware over time of how 
their cities are evolving and how their participation impacts this 
evolution. Thus, visual analytics are introduced in the approach to 
offer a visual credibility to citizens,  since that  the quality of CP 
processes depends on the quality of the data provided to citizens 
[6]. Nevertheless, if data is not true, accurate, complete and 
timely, visualisations tools would not reflect realities. Hence, we 
can say that depending on the quality of information  collected, 
analyzed and offered to citizens , spatio -temporal visualization 
could enhance/reduce citizens’ trust in governments. 
Consequently, citizens should perceive the e -participation process 
as a transparent and trustful process, otherwise they may not be 
involved and may not impact as expected the decisions. Hence, 
the e -participation solution has to provide tools for citizens to 
materialize this transparency and this trust. GIS and Visualization 
tools are solutions that can enhance this transparency and trust t o 
citizens by prov iding tools to visualize projects, decisions, and 
collected data, from the beginning of the process until the taken 
decisions. Citizens can visualize data in two perspectives: time 
and space. Moreover, as stated by  [43], transparency is n ot just 
about the quantity of information provided to citizens or the way 
by which it is presented, but also about its accuracy, usability, 
completeness, integrity etc.  Thus, the output of the visualization 
tools depend s on the quality of data provided to these tools. 
Hence, the proposed model is based on: " context-based 
reasoning", " visual analytics " and " spatio-temporal 
representation" (see Figure 2).  
Overall, the use of citizens ’ contexts, their involvement along all 
the process from its beginning to it  end, enhanced with spati o-
temporal visualization tools, should increase the social  
acceptability of participatory processes [57]. Consequently, 
context-based reasoning (C xBR) techniques can be used to feed 
the visualization tool. C xBR will reflect the information related to 
the citizen’s living context since this information is provided by 
citizens themselves in an attempt to reflect, as well as possible, 
real problems and priorities [27].

5. Discussion  
Although public participation outstands a great value for cities 
and communities, its implementation raises several challenges and 
issues [2, 9]. In this paper, we tried to depict issues related to CP 
processes regarding two major acto rs who are citizens and 
organisations. We recognize that other issues from both sides may 
exist but these ones emerge from a first overview of the literature . 
A typology of issues has emerged in this paper depicting six major 
categories of issues (see figu re 1). In this part of the paper, we 
will discuss three major challenges at higher level of abstraction 
regarding CP that are: Governance, the multidisciplinary nature of 
CP and the vulnerability of CP. In what follows, we present an 
overview of the three depicted challenges of CP. We recognize 
that these three challenges are not exhaustive but may constitute 
main challenges that researchers could focus on.    
Governance:  
Several studies have been conducted to show how CP impacts 
governance systems  [52]. [2] consider that CP is being 
increasingly adopted, given the central place it takes in the 
development agendas both locally and internationally. In this 
context, through a review of CP initiatives that took place at the 
European Union (EU) during the last decade, [52] argue the trend 

of current governance systems to rely on participatory approaches. 
However, and according to these authors, the main challenge is to 
maintain a productive tension in a participatory process that  is 
both "system-oriented" and "actor-driven". In fact, citizens should 
take a leading role in order to make their voice heard by decision -
makers. Hence, CP  can be seen as a melting point between 
citizens and decision -makers. It changes the way citizens 
contribute to their society by brin ging their ideas and their 
opinions and it impacts the way decisions are taken by decision -
makers who now have a new source of input, that is the citizens’ 
voice, and that was not considered before. Hence, CP impacts the 
governance systems by which decisio ns are taken.  In this paper, 
several issues regarding the implementing of CP processes have 
been identified (see section 2) , showing that governance systems 
that rely on participatory approaches should consider these issues 
in the design of their approache s and incorporate strategies that fit 
with both citizens and organizations’ needs. 
The multi-disciplinary nature of Citizen participation: 
Citizens’ participation i s a multi -disciplinary concept [20, 46, 9]. 
In fact, CP can be studied from the point of vie w of different 
fields such as, the types of discourses associated with CP 
("Normative, Interpretive, Critique and Dialogic"), the 
technologies that have direct relevance to e -Participation 
(including: Geographic information systems , Decision Support 
Systems, and visualization), the research themes (including: e -
Government and e-Democracy), and reference disciplines that are 
part of research on citizen participation (including: "Information 
Systems, Social and political ph ilosophy and Computer Science")  
[53].  
Actually, one of the factors leading to the complexity of public 
participation implementation could be its multidisciplinary nature.  
Considering the various research disciplines, themes, technologies  
related to this concept, elaboration of interdiscipli nary studies 
appears to be a promising avenue suggesting many opportunities 
to enhance the field. Thus, m any authors suggest that 
“interdisciplinary” [45] or “transdisciplinary” [20] research could 
consolidate the relevance of the field.  An interdisciplina ry 
dialogue would allow a multidimensional analysis of issues and 
challenges, which could help researchers and practitioners to 
better understand the needs and goals of citizens and 
organizations in terms of participation.  The typology of issues 
proposed i n this paper confirms and argues the multidisciplinary 
nature of the field (through the different categories of issues it 
depicts; political, technological, ethical , cost -effectiveness, 
citizens and quality issues ). Second, it opens up new research 
avenues since it could be  taken as a start point to study the issues 
as well as the links that could exist between them. Moreover, it 
could be used by scholars from several disciplines to reinforce the 
understanding of issues and to propose interdisciplinary 
approaches that would enhance CP.  
Vulnerability 
One of the main challenges found in the literature is that CP is not 
used as it was intended but as a mean to support decisions that 
were already taken. In this context , notions of “ manipulative 
participation” and “passive participation ” [15], or “citizen as 
spectator” [19, 60] have been evoked to reflect a passive 
participation in decision -making process and to reflect formal 
participatory processes that aim to legitimate decisions already 
taken by powerful acto rs within society.  In addition, several 
authors depicted potential risks that could result from 
inappropriate CP processes such as people marginalization, social 
inequities and unjust economic and political arrangements that all 
lead to biased results [26, 28, 30, 38]. Drawing on the typology of 
issues proposed in this paper, a new approach for CP is proposed 
combining two categories of issues (citizens’ and technological 
issues). This approach aims to enable effective citizen 
participation processes that w ould help both citizens and 
governments to understand real problems and priorities and to 
create added value from citizens’ opinions. Actually, as argued in 
sections 3.2.4 and 3.2.5, among barriers to citizen participation 
success, the inadequate understan ding of real problematics and 
the deal with topics that are distant from people’s daily problems. 
For that, it becomes important that both the research and practice 
communities be aware of these challenges and work to understand 
and overcome them so that C P objectives can be reached without 
any bias. 
6. Conclusion 
In this research paper, a literature review was conducted in order 
to identify major issues and challenges related to citizens’ 
participation. A typology of issues has emerged including six 
major categories that are ethical, efficiency and cost-effectiveness, 
political, quality, citizens , and technology. Drawing on two 
categories that are “citizens” and “technology” , a context -based 
approach was proposed in an attempt to better respond to needs 
and goals of both citizens and organisations. Mainly, this 
approach is based on three major concepts that are context-based 
reasoning, spatio-temporal representation  and visual analytics . 
Each concept was initially selected to respond to one or more 
issues in  an attempt to enhance citizen participation and 
involvement. As stated above, our approach is a citizen -centric 
one. Therefore, the context -based reasoning was identified as a 
key concept that could greatly enhance the participatory approach. 
Moreover, spatio-temporal representation and visual analytics  
were introduced to offer better visibility on decisions and 
processes and to enable a spatio-temporal evolution  regarding 
projects and initiatives.   
As future work, we identify three research directions. F irst, we 
need to determine the elements of the context that citizens will fill 
in and that will be integrated with the e -participation platform. To 
this end, qualitative and quantitative  studies with citizens and 
decision-makers can be conducted in order t o determine this 
contextual data. Second, the GIS engine has to be built. The GIS 
will be augmented with the context information collected from 
citizens and with context -based reasoning techniques that will 
help extract knowledge from citizens’ context and  combine the 
context with the input data from citizens. Third, the visualization 
tool needs to be implemented and encompass the evolution of the 
inputs and of the contexts with regard to two dimensions: time 
and space. Finally, this platform needs to be de veloped and tested 
in order to be evaluated and to see if it really overcome the 
different issues identified in the literature.
