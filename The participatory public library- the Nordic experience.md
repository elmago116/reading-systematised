From access to user participation

Concluding remarks
There is no doubt that Web 2.0 has had a major impact on the development of
participatoryactivitiesinlibraries,butasitappearsfromthetextabove,participatory
activities do not necessarily have to take place in a digital context. As described, there
are not only many examples of participatory culture in the physical library, but these
activitiesarealsoverydiverse.Themeaningofparticipationas take partcanbeseenas

workshops or being involved in library development. Furthermore, there are many
reasons for working towards a participatory library. Sometimes participation is
regardedasawaytorescueachallengedlibrary,whereasothertimes,itisperceivedas
societal glue or as a fulfilment of user needs. These diverse arguments combined with
the many different forms of participation make the participatory library a complex

Today,participationisabuzzword,butuserparticipationinpubliclibrariesalso
raisessomecriticalquestions.Firstly,isitpossibleforpubliclibrariestoimplement
differentkindsofuserparticipationwhenbudgetsarereduced?Ontheonehand,the
optimist can claim that user participation and volunteerism are overlapping

management of user participation, just as volunteerism, is a time-consuming task
for library professionals. Furthermore, if volunteers should not be viewed as just
unpaidworkers,butalsoasagroupofsuperusers,thevolunteers’“work”ismainly

maybe reasonable arguments for user participation in libraries are not sufficient in
